Every shipping container on Earth has the same steel corners. In 1937, trucker Malcolm McLean waited all day at a New Jersey dock, as workers unloaded his cotton bales, one by one. So, he wondered... why not lift the whole truck onto the ship? But, wheels and cabs wasted precious space. So, he took the boxes off the wheels, and in 1956, an old tanker sailed with fifty-eight of them. Loading costs fell from nearly six dollars a ton, to sixteen cents. But, every company built different boxes, with different hooks. A crane in one port couldn't lift a box from another. So, engineer Keith Tantlinger designed a steel corner with an oval hole, and a twistlock that turns, and locks. But, rivals would have to pay to use it. So, McLean gave the patents away, for free. Now, one crane could lift any box, onto any ship, truck, or train. Today, almost everything you own once rode in a steel box, locked at the same steel corners.
